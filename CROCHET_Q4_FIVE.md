# Five crochet patterns for a $700+ Q4

Researched against live Etsy market pages on **Oct 3, 2026**. Every "evidence" line below is a
real listing with its review count and price as displayed that day; review count is a *proxy*
for units sold (Etsy publishes no volumes), and it is labelled as such. Prices are USD.

Format is machine-checked by `verify_five.py`: exactly 13 tags per listing, every tag <= 20
chars, every title <= 140 chars, and every `REALISE` equal to `LIST` minus `DISC`.

### The net formula behind every price here

`net = realised - $0.20 listing - 6.5% transaction - (3% + $0.25) processing`
i.e. `net = 0.905 x realised - 0.45`. Fees are ~13% at $12, ~19% at $4.70. Small tickets are
where a digital shop dies, so nothing below is priced under $9.99 realised.

## The three rules that make these five work

**1. Every niche gets a three-tier ladder.** Single $6.99-9.99 / set $12-18 / everything $32. The
middle tier is the one that sells, and its job is to make the top tier look reasonable. Right now
your shop has one tier - $4.50-11.99 - which is why your average order is $4.70.

**2. Video is what converts a PDF from $2 to $12.** Three of the five incumbents that hold a
double-digit price put **"+VIDEO"** or **"+VIDEO + App"** in the title. Nobody paying $12.80 for
three patterns is paying for the text; they are paying because it looks taught. A 30-second
flip-through on your phone is the whole difference, and it earns the listing the video badge in
search results.

**3. Never discount into the ground at launch.** Your shop's `On sale: 36 of 36` is why a $15.99
planner looks like a $12 product. These five launch at a scheduled discount with an end date
(F1-F5 above), and go back to full price after. A permanent sale is not a sale, it is a lower
price with worse optics.

## One warning worth more than the five picks

A **"Grinch Advent Calendar Crochet Pattern"** with 63 reviews is sitting on page 1 of the advent
search at $3.90. Grinch is a Dr. Seuss trademark; Winnie the Pooh, Kpop dolls, Disney and Bluey
pattern listings get pulled in waves, and the takedown deletes the listing *with its reviews* and
can restrict the shop. Licensed characters are the fastest way to look like you are selling and
the fastest way to lose the listing that carries your Q4. All five picks above are generic
subject matter and are safe.

**What "verified" means here:** `python3 verify_patterns.py` recomputes every round and row from the
line before it — consumes, produces, the stitch chain across rounds, the multiplier arithmetic, the
declared motif/figure counts against the blocks actually present, the required front matter, and it
refuses to pass a pattern that claims testers or hand-edited charts. It caught 26 bad rounds in the
nativity file and three in the stockings file, including a real pattern bug (a 6-to-12 increase
written as `(1 sc, inc) x6`, which yields 18). It also caught my own editing note leaking into a
pattern line, which is exactly the failure mode a checker exists for.

**What it does not mean:** that the fabric looks right, that the gauge table matches your yarn, that
a camel reads as a camel, or that a buyer will not email you about the reindeer antlers. Those are
hand-and-eye problems and only a hook solves them. The files say `TEST_STATUS: untested` and the
listing copy must keep saying that too — do not add "tested by", "tech edited" or "proofed by" to
anything, because you have no testers yet and a pattern review is the one review that ruins a
crochet shop.

**To export:** `python3 make_charts.py && python3 make_pdfs.py` writes print-ready HTML into
`dist/patterns/`. Open one, Ctrl/Cmd-P, Save as PDF, Letter or A4, background graphics ticked. That
is your Etsy file. The chart PDFs are separate uploads on purpose — `AZ-name-chart` is the reason
someone pays $11.99 instead of $1.62.
