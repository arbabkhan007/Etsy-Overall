# F3 · 24-mini December countdown garland

**Product:** 24 mini amigurumi motifs, a numbers chart (1-24) and 24 printable gift-note slips.
**Sell as:** `Crochet Advent Calendar Pattern, 24 Mini Motif Countdown Garland, Numbers Chart and Gift Notes, Christmas Amigurumi PDF, Beginner` — list $18.99, 30% off, realise $13.29.
**TEST_STATUS:** untested draft — crochet M01, M02 and M07 end to end before you publish any of it.
**BABY_SAFE:** no — these are ornaments; state "not a toy for children under 3" in the listing.

Every stitch count in this file is machine-checked by `verify_patterns.py`: each round consumes
exactly the stitches the previous round made and produces exactly the number printed after the
em dash. That removes the error class that gets a new pattern shop its first 1-star review. It
does not test gauge, look or feel.

## Materials

- Acrylic DK / 8-ply (Sport-Worsted boundary), approx 20 g total for all 24 minis in 4 colours:
  red, green, cream, gold. Leftover-friendly by design — none of these need more than 6 g.
- Hook 3.0 mm (US E/4) for motifs, 3.5 mm for chains.
- 9 mm safety eyes (optional; M07, M08, M09, M14, M23) plus a darning needle.
- Fibrefill stuffing, approx 15 g.
- 24 wooden or paper beads numbered 1-24, or the printed tags described in Numbers chart.
- 1.2 m of twine or ribbon for the garland, plus 24 tiny clothespins (buyer supplies; say so).

## Gauge

10 sc × 12 rnds = 2.5 cm (1 in) worked in the round, 3.0 mm hook, DK acrylic.
Finished motifs measure 3.5-5 cm. If your gauge is loose, drop a hook size before adding rounds —
stuffing pushing through is the single most common complaint on mini amigurumi reviews.

## Yarn and substitutions

Every motif is a scrap-eater, 2-8 g each; the only skein worth buying is the ground colour for the
garland cord and the numerals. Buy hooks by millimetres: "size 7" reads 4.5 mm in the United States
and 3.0 mm on an old English hook, and a 24-piece garland crocheted one size down is 15% smaller
than the photo, which is the review you do not want.

| what the pattern calls for | US shelves | UK shelves |
|---|---|---|
| Fingering / 1 ply, 3.0-3.5 mm | Knit Picks Palette, Lion Brand Lace Weight | Sirdar Meirion Lace, James C.F. Riviera 4 ply |
| Sport / 2 ply, 3.5 mm | Bernat Softee Baby | Stylecraft Special Sport, Paintbox Cotton DK (smaller hook) |
| DK for the sturdier versions | Lion Brand Wool-Ease DK | Stylecraft Special DK, King Cole Merino Blend DK |

Cotton holds a block shape and does not fuzz in a gift box; acrylic is cheaper and forgives a
tension that varies over 24 pieces, which it will.

## Care

Wipe the numerals with a dry cloth. If the garland lives on a banister, take it down in February
and store it flat in a box with the ends unfastened, because a year of creasing is what makes a
second-December garland look tired. Steam from a distance if a motif has been crushed; never press
an iron onto acrylic.

## How these are built (read before starting)

- All closed motifs are worked in a **continuous spiral**; put a marker in the first stitch of
  each round or the numbers will drift.
- `x6` means repeat the bracketed instruction six times around.
- Stuff firmly for spheres, lightly for flat pieces. A mini that sags at the seam reads cheap in
  the photo that sells the whole pattern.
- Five motifs (M03, M05, M06, M24 and the antlers of M08) are chain-based and marked freeform:
  they carry no stitch math by design, so they are also the easiest to improvise colours on.
- Change yarn at a `dec` round where possible; the decrease hides the join.

## The 24 motifs

### M01 · Mini stocking (flat, seamed)

Row 1  ch 10, 9 sc across — 9
Rows 2-9  ch 1 and turn, sc 9 — 9
Row 10  ch 1 and turn, inc, sc 7, inc — 11
Row 11  ch 1 and turn, inc, sc 9, inc — 13
Rows 12-17  ch 1 and turn, sc 13 — 13
Row 18  ch 1 and turn, dec, sc 9, dec — 11
Row 19  ch 1 and turn, dec, sc 7, dec — 9
Rows 20-21  ch 1 and turn, sc 9 — 9
Finish: seam the toe and the back of the leg with a ladder stitch, stuff the leg only. Cuff: join
cream yarn at the top corner, work 9 sc in blo along the opening, then 3 more rows of 9 sc;
fasten off. Weave a 12 cm twine loop through the cuff corner.

### M02 · Mini tree

R1  MR, 6 sc — 6
R2  (inc) x6 — 12
R3  (1 sc, inc) x6 — 18
R4  (2 sc, inc) x6 — 24
R5  (3 sc, inc) x6 — 30
R6  (4 sc, inc) x6 — 36
R7-R12  sc around — 36
R13  (10 sc, dec) x3 — 33
Finish: stuff the cone firmly through the base, close the base with 6 evenly spaced dec to 30 and
fasten off with a long tail; cinch the opening shut. Works upturned, so the tip stays crisp. Pin
the base flat on a towel and mist it once to set the silhouette.

### M03 · Star (freeform)

freeform — 5-point chain star: MR with 5 ch-arms: (ch 5, sl st in 2nd ch from hook, sl st in ring)
repeated 5 times, then sc 4 along each arm with a (sc, ch 1, sc) corner at the tip; fill the centre
with 12 sc into the ring. Lay flat and block under a book for 10 minutes.

### M04 · Bell

R1  MR, 6 sc — 6
R2  (inc) x6 — 12
R3  (1 sc, inc) x6 — 18
R4-R6  sc around — 18
R7  (2 sc, inc) x6 — 24
R8  (3 sc, inc) x6 — 30
R9  (4 sc, inc) x6 — 36
R10-R13  sc around — 36
Finish: leave open. Clapper: ch 6 with a 4 mm bead threaded on it, draw up through the top and
knot inside. Hanging loop: ch 6, sl st into the tip round. Pin the flare out and block before
photographing; an unblocked bell looks like a lump.

### M05 · Candy cane (freeform)

freeform — ch 18 in white. Round 1: sc in the 2nd ch from hook and in each ch to the last 5; then
work the hook: (2 sc, ch 2, 2 sc) into the last ch, continue sc down the other side of the chain;
sl st to join. Change to red every 2 sc for 8 stripes, carried up the back of the work.

### M06 · Snowflake (freeform)

freeform — ch 5, sl st into ring. 6 arms: (ch 5, sc in 2nd ch, sc, dc, sl st back at ring) six
times, then a second layer of 6 shorter arms (ch 3, sl st) worked between them. Stiffen by
soaking in 1:1 white glue and water, pin flat, dry overnight.

## Gift notes - print, cut, tuck inside

The slip is what turns a garland into a calendar: each motif hides a numbered note, and the note is
the actual gift on most days. Print this page single-sided at 100%, cut on the lines, fold once. The
prompt is printed on the front and the inside stays blank for you.

| day | prompt on the slip |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Wake someone you forgot - the neighbour who took in your parcels |
| Day 2 | Buy the thing they keep re-marking online - before it sells out |
| Day 3 | Say thanks for one specific thing - from this year, out loud |
| Day 4 | Give an experience, not an object - two tickets, no wrapping needed |
| Day 5 | Re-gift joyfully - the candle you love but own three of |
| Day 6 | Ask what they already have - and buy the upgrade, not the duplicate |
| Day 7 | Send something by post - to the friend who lives far away |
| Day 8 | Make the day easier - a paid-for breakfast on the 25th |
| Day 9 | Give the luxury size - of one thing they only buy in minis |
| Day 10 | Fund a small obsession - they would never buy themselves |
| Day 11 | Write the note first - then choose the gift to match it |
| Day 12 | Two of the same thing - one for them, one for you, on purpose |
| Day 13 | Skip the toy - give the storage for the toys instead |
| Day 14 | Bring back a tradition - the board game everyone outgrew |
| Day 15 | Buy time - a cleaning session, a meal kit, a lift to the airport |
| Day 16 | Give the framed version - of a photo that has lived on a phone |
| Day 17 | Something that keeps - a tool, not a decoration |
| Day 18 | Feed them - the good tin of biscuits, the one you ration |
| Day 19 | Wrap it in a towel - they will use, and knot it, not tape it |
| Day 20 | Give the next level - of the hobby they only dabble in |

Two rules that make these land. Keep every prompt under a line, because a slip with three sentences
reads as homework. And write the gift on Day 24 yourself, in ink, before December starts, because
that is the one everybody forgets while they are wrapping.

## Numbers chart

Two styles, both in `AZ-name-chart.md` alongside the letters so the whole garland is consistent:

- **Cross-stitch numerals** 1-24 on the stocking cuff, worked on the same grid as the letters (see the generated chart).
- **Appliqué numerals** cut from felt or chained in surface slip stitch, for the motifs you cannot
  sew a chart onto (M18 wreath, M15 present).
Generate or edit the grid with `python3 make_charts.py`; do not hand-type the grids, they will
drift a row and the whole chart looks wrong.

## Finishing & assembly

- Weave in tails on the inside, then a drop of fabric glue on any tail that sits at a stuffing
  gap. Mini amigurumi get handled more than full-size ones.
- Stuff firmly: a mini that dents when you pinch it photographs badly and refunds.
- Open the top of M01, M11, M15, M19 and M20 before assembly - each holds a gift-note slip, and a
  sealed one is a mistake buyers find in the photo reviews.
- Every motif gets a 4 cm loop: ch 8 sewn through the top stitch, or a 12 cm twine thread through
  the whole garland with a knot behind each motif.
- Give the buyer both: a 24-bead version (thread the motifs on one line) and a 12-pair version
  (two garlands, one for a mantel, one for a door) - the pair version is the one people actually
  make in time for December 1.
